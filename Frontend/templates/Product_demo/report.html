Executive Summary

Markets are primarily driven by a severe escalation in geopolitical tensions between the United States and Iran, which forms the central narrative. Critical news of Iranian threats, a reported attack on a US base, and US intelligence assessing a high risk of imminent retaliation have injected a significant risk premium into oil prices. This bullish momentum for crude is being challenged by counter-narratives from former President Trump, who is attempting to jawbone prices lower and ordering an increase in drilling. In the background, economic data paints a picture of divergence, with strong US PMI figures suggesting economic resilience. Conversely, mixed-to-weak PMI data from Europe and the UK creates headwinds for their respective currencies, further bolstering the US dollar as a safe-haven asset. Ultimately, the immediate threat of conflict is overshadowing economic fundamentals and is the key determinant of market sentiment.

Market Assessment

The market is operating in a heightened state of alert, with the US-Iran conflict acting as the primary catalyst for price action. The escalating threats and military actions have established a significant war premium in oil (OIL.WTI), creating extreme volatility. This is evidenced by the tug-of-war between news of imminent Iranian retaliation and US political intervention aimed at capping prices. While the threat of conflict is the dominant force, a clear economic divergence is also at play. Stronger-than-expected US PMI data contrasts with lackluster European figures, supporting a stronger USD. This dynamic, coupled with a flight to safety, positions the dollar favorably against the Euro and Pound Sterling, whose own economic outlooks appear fragile based on their respective PMI releases.
